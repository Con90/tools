# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

desktop

Linux desktop only — GTK 3 + pycairo, X11/Wayland with a compositing window
manager. Not `web`, `ios`, `android`, or `adaptive`; none of the schema's four
values describes this app, and recording one of them would misroute future
work. No cross-platform port and no phone or web companion is planned.

## Users

One user: the app's author, on their own Linux desktop (Mint / Cinnamon
lineage). Not built for distribution to strangers, though `install.sh` and the
README make it cloneable. Design decisions may assume this machine, this
desktop environment, and this person's taste — there is no unknown-user
audience to accommodate.

The situation: working at the desktop, wanting a bounded stretch of focus, with
the timer living on the wallpaper rather than in a window to be managed.

## Product Purpose

A Pomodoro timer for real focus practice, whose interface is a harbour seal
hauled out on the desktop. The seal is not decoration applied to a timer — it
is the timer: the countdown sits on its flank, remaining time drains down its
body as a coloured level line, its eyes open while running and squint when
paused, and completed sessions show as dots beneath the clock.

Success is that a focus session is started, run to the end, and read at a
glance without the timer ever becoming a window to manage.

## Positioning

Every other Pomodoro app is a window, a panel applet, or a tray icon. This one
is a shaped, frameless, transparent creature on the wallpaper with no chrome at
all. The whole animal is the control surface — click to start, drag to move,
scroll to adjust, hover to reveal controls that otherwise do not exist on
screen. That absence of a box is the product, not a styling choice.

## Operating Context

- Runs continuously on the desktop, typically always-on-top, positioned once
  and left there. Window position and pinned state persist.
- Interaction is direct manipulation on the seal itself: click (start/pause),
  drag (move), scroll (±1 minute while paused), hover (reveal the six control
  chips), right-click (menu). Keyboard when focused: `space` `r` `s` `t` `,` `q`.
- Sits over arbitrary wallpaper, so contrast cannot be assumed from any
  background.
- End-of-session feedback reaches the user through the seal (bounce, flipper
  clap, sparkles), plus optional `paplay` sound and `notify-send` notification.
- Settings, position and pin state live in
  `~/.config/seal-pomodoro/config.json`.

## Capabilities and Constraints

Confirmed capabilities: focus / short break / long break cycle with configurable
lengths and sessions-per-long-break; start, pause, reset, skip; per-minute
adjustment while paused; always-on-top toggle; seal size; end-of-session sound
and notification; auto-start of the next session.

**Hard constraints — future work must not break these:**

- **Single file.** The app stays `seal_pomodoro.py`. No package, no module
  split, no build step.
- **Frameless shaped window.** It must never become an ordinary window with a
  titlebar or panel. The rectangular dark-background path under a
  non-compositing WM is a degraded fallback, not an alternative design.

**Not hard constraints, but currently true and load-bearing enough that
changing them is a deliberate decision, not a side effect:**

- The seal is drawn entirely in cairo — no image assets. `make_icon.py` renders
  `icons/` from the app's own drawing code, so the icon and the app cannot
  drift apart. Introducing bitmap assets would sever that.
- Dependencies are Python 3, PyGObject (GTK 3) and pycairo only — what ships by
  default on Mint/Ubuntu. `paplay` and `notify-send` are optional and degrade
  quietly.

**Scope, as decided:** the Pomodoro loop as built is complete. Session history,
stats, streaks, and task/project labels are explicitly out of scope. The one
direction that could still earn a place is *distraction discipline* — something
with teeth: noticing abandoned sessions, resisting casual pausing, noticing
when the user has stopped. Undecided in form; not to be invented without asking.

## Brand Commitments

- Name: **Seal Pomodoro**. App id `seal-pomodoro`, WM class `Seal-pomodoro`.
- The subject is a **harbour seal**, hauled out belly-down: head raised at the
  right, body tapering left, rear flippers lifted clear of the ground. Not a
  generic cartoon animal, not a mascot logo.
- Accent colours carry session meaning: orange = focus, green = short break,
  blue = long break.
- README voice is plain, concrete and unhyped ("No frame, no panel, no box").

## Evidence on Hand

- `seal_pomodoro.py` — the whole app, including all seal geometry and drawing.
- `icons/` — SVG + PNGs at 9 sizes, generated by `make_icon.py`.
- `install.sh` — icon theme install, menu entry, trusted desktop launcher.
- `README.md` — accurate current description of behaviour and requirements.
- No users beyond the author, no usage data, no testimonials, no benchmarks,
  no release channel. Future work must not fabricate any of these.

## Product Principles

1. **The animal is the interface.** Every control, readout and state change
   should be expressed through the seal's own anatomy and behaviour before
   reaching for added UI. Chrome pasted onto the seal is a failure.
2. **No box, ever.** The value is a creature on the wallpaper. Anything that
   pushes toward a conventional window erodes the entire premise.
3. **Legibility and charm are not a trade.** The author rejects the framing
   that one must cost the other: if the seal ever makes the remaining time
   harder to find, that is a design problem to solve, not a price to pay.
4. **It has to survive any wallpaper.** Contrast, halos and rims are
   correctness requirements, not polish, because the background is unknown.
5. **The loop is finished; depth beats addition.** Improvements make the
   existing session loop more reliable, more readable and more alive. New
   feature surface needs a reason, and only discipline-with-teeth has standing.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No product-specific standard established. The single relevant known need is
glanceable legibility of remaining time from normal seating distance, over
arbitrary wallpaper — covered under Product Principles.
